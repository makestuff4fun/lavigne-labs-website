Most people think Taobao is where you buy a phone case. I use it to buy laser-cut and bent parts, custom cables and custom LCDs, straight from the people who make them. Around here they call me 淘宝王, the king of Taobao. It started as an inside joke, and it stuck, because it is more or less true: I can find almost anything on Taobao, and I can usually get a good deal on it. Today the king is sharing one of his best-kept tricks.

I have spent roughly $1M on Taobao over the years. I am in the highest customer tier. I have had relationships with shops I found there that have lasted many years, with excellent service the whole way. And yes, you can still visit a shop or a factory in person. I have.

So when people ask whether Taobao is a serious place to find suppliers, I don't hedge. It is.

## What matters is the supplier

Everyone asks the wrong question. They ask which platform is best: Alibaba.com, 1688, Taobao. The right question is how good the supplier is. A great workshop is great wherever you found it, and a bad one is bad wherever you found it. The platform is just a door.

Taobao happens to be a very good door, because it is where a huge number of small Chinese businesses already sell. Some of them are specialised in ways that surprise people. My favourite example: a custom LCD supplier. Not a catalogue panel, a panel made for your product. I found it the same way you would find a phone case, by typing a few words into a search box. Specialised does not mean hidden behind a trade-show booth and an English brochure. Often it just means a small team that is very good at one thing and has a shop page.

## Why the small ones are the good ones

A shop on Taobao is cheap to run. No booth, no export sales team, no polished catalogue. A small workshop with a few machines can post photos of what it makes, set a price, and answer messages. That is enough.

And the people behind those shops are often very happy to have your business. A small factory depends on only a few clients, so each one matters. You are not order number four thousand in a queue. Your drawing gets looked at by someone who cares whether you come back. The smaller operation is frequently the better partner, and the better price, because there is no sales machine to pay for.

That is where the many-years relationships came from. You send a drawing, they do it right, you pay on time, and a year later they are quietly fixing your problems before you notice them.

Things you can find this way include:

- **Raw-material suppliers**: sheet, bar and extrusion, often cut to length.
- **Custom heatsinks**: cut and machined to your drawing from existing profiles. A brand-new extrusion needs its own die, so that is a tooling job with a bigger order behind it.
- **Laser cutting and bending**: send a file, get flat or formed parts back.
- **Custom wire harnesses and cables**: made to your length, connector and pinout.
- **Connectors**: from common catalogue parts to less common ones.
- **Displays and other specialised parts**: yes, including the custom LCD.

## How the three platforms differ

**Alibaba.com** is export-facing. The listings are in English, the sales reps speak English, and that is the convenience. It is also the cost. I call it Brian's Law:

> **"If the factory can deal with you in English, you can find a lower price."**

It is a rule of thumb, not a physical law, but it explains why the English-friendly platform is rarely the cheapest one.

**1688** is Alibaba's domestic wholesale site. It is Chinese-language and aimed at buyers who order in volume, so minimum order quantities often don't suit a prototype run.

**Taobao** is built for individual buyers, so many shops will sell you one piece. For a sample or a small first order, that matters a great deal.

## How to search

Search in Chinese, and search by process, not by product. A few terms to start with:

- **激光切割** (jīguāng qiēgē): laser cutting
- **折弯** (zhéwān): bending, usually sheet-metal bending
- **散热器定制** (sànrèqì dìngzhì): custom heatsinks
- **线束加工** (xiànshù jiāgōng): wire harness processing or assembly
- **连接器** (liánjiēqì): connectors
- **型材** (xíngcái): profiles, meaning extrusions
- **板材** (bǎncái): sheet or plate material

Add the material or a dimension and see what comes up. Combine terms too: laser cutting plus bending is often sold together.

**From search to shortlist.** Run each term with your material, then open the shops, not just the listings. Sort out the traders from the workshops: a workshop shows machines, offcuts, a bench, real parts with burrs and tooling marks; a trader shows glossy renders and a long list of unrelated products. Open a good handful of shops, keep the few that look like they make the thing themselves, and drop the rest. Check how long the shop has been open and what the reviews actually say about repeat orders. Then do the same search in 1688 and compare what the same process costs there. If you don't read Chinese, a translation tool is enough to get started, but check any technical term with a native speaker before you order.

## Read the listing like a quote

Many process listings are priced per piece, with options for material, thickness or length. Treat that as a starting point, not a final quote. Look for photos of real work rather than stock images, and for shops that show their machines, not only finished parts. Check what the price includes: is the cutting file work free, is bending priced separately, is there a setup charge on small quantities, is the material grade stated or only "stainless". Vague options usually mean vague answers later. Reviews help, but read them with the buyer in mind: a review for a phone case tells you nothing about tolerance.

## Get behind the shop

The shop is the front door. Taobao buyers talk to sellers through **旺旺** (Aliwangwang), the built-in chat. Message the shop, send your drawing, and ask direct questions. A first message that works is short and concrete, in Chinese: what you want made, the material, the quantity, a drawing as PDF plus the native file, and one question per line:

- Do you make this yourselves, or is it passed on to another workshop?
- What tolerance can you hold on this feature?
- Can you quote a small batch, and what changes at a larger one?
- How long to make, and how will it be packed?

A friendly, specific answer from the person at the machines is how you find out there is a real factory behind the listing, and that it will quote you a proper price. Slow, vague or copy-pasted replies tell you something too. When the chat gets complicated, a phone call clears up in minutes what takes a day of typing.

Judge them the way you would judge any supplier. Ask for photos or video of your kind of work, and don't accept "no problem" as the answer to every requirement. When it matters, go and see them. The good ones are glad to be visited.

## Order samples cheaply

If shops sell by the piece, a sample costs very little compared with a trip or a minimum order elsewhere. Order a few, measure them, test the fit, and compare. Run the same drawing through two or three shops and you learn quickly who holds tolerances and who doesn't. Say plainly in the order note that it is a sample and what you will check. On arrival, photograph the box before you open it, then the parts with a scale in frame, and measure the critical dimensions with calipers, noting hole positions, bend angles and flatness. Send your feedback back through the chat with the photos: how a shop reacts to "this hole is off" tells you more than the part does. Then place a second, slightly larger order with the one that handled it well. That is how a relationship starts.

## Paying, and getting it out of China

**Alipay.** Taobao pays through Alipay. Foreign buyers can usually link an international Visa or Mastercard to Alipay, after identity verification, and pay that way. If that does not work for you, a trusted payer in China can place and pay for the order. Either way, pay through the platform, not by sending money to a stranger. The platform holds your payment until you confirm receipt, and that protection is the point.

**Forwarders (集运).** If a shop ships only inside China, ship to a forwarder's warehouse in China and let them send it on. Give the shop the forwarder's address and your forwarder ID in the address line, and ask for a domestic tracking number. Tell the forwarder what is inside, the real value and material, and ask them to consolidate several shops into one box. It is cheaper than several small international parcels, and you can ask for photos of the parts before they leave.

**Invoices (发票).** Ask before you pay, not after. A registered company can issue a proper invoice; a small individual shop often cannot, or only in a limited form. That does not make an individual shop bad, and I buy from both, but if you need the paperwork for accounting or customs, it decides whom you can use.

**Returns and disputes.** If a part is wrong, open a refund or return request in the order, not just in the chat, and keep the chat record, photos and your drawing. If the two of you cannot agree, the platform can step in and look at the evidence. Do not click confirm receipt until you have inspected the parts, because that releases the money.

## The limits

I'd be doing you a disservice if I stopped there. This route has real friction:

- **You need Chinese.** Listings, chat and drawing notes are in Chinese, and misunderstanding a spec is easy.
- **Accounts and payment.** Taobao is built for buyers inside China. Whether a foreign buyer can open an account and pay with a foreign card has changed over time, so check before relying on it. Many foreign buyers end up using a local contact to place the order.
- **Shipping out of China.** Many shops ship only within China. Check whether you need a forwarder and what customs treatment you will face.
- **Quality varies.** A shop can be excellent or poor, and the listing won't tell you which. Inspect samples before you scale up.
- **Not everything fits.** Anything safety-critical, certified or tightly toleranced deserves proper factory vetting, not just a chat window.

None of that changes the main point. It means the work is in finding the right supplier, which is exactly the work worth doing anywhere.

## So, is it worth it?

For prototypes, small runs and custom parts like cut and bent sheet, heatsinks, cables and displays, absolutely. For larger programs, use Taobao to discover the right kind of workshop, then build a proper supplier relationship with them.

Be honest about the friction, though. It means working in Chinese, getting an account and payment that work, sometimes picking up the phone, and sometimes standing in the shop. Trust takes time too: mine took years to build, one good order at a time.
